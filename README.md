# Farmhouse Holdout

*Kessler Farm. Board up. Prepare for the horde. Survive as long as you can.*

Farmhouse Holdout is an isometric zombie survival roguelike that runs in your browser. You hold an old farmhouse
against wave after wave of the dead. Between waves you nail boards over the windows, craft ammo, upgrade your guns
and patch yourself up. When a wave is cleared you pick one card to make your run stronger. Play alone, or with up to
three friends online.

---

## How a run goes

1. **Break.** The clock is stopped. Board up the windows and doors, spend scrap at the stations and get into
   position. Press **T** when you're ready, or wait out the timer.
2. **Wave.** The zombies come out of the trees, break your boards down and climb in. Keep them out, and keep moving.
3. **Cards.** When the last zombie falls, pick one of three cards: a new gun, a skill, a perk or supplies. Every fifth
   wave brings a bonus hand. Or take a little scrap instead, a bit more each wave. Whatever's still lying about glides
   in to you once the cards are done.
   - Now and then a hand comes with a **sacrifice**. Give up three quarters of your scrap and three more cards are
     dealt (the rarer ones likelier), and you take two of the six.
   - Rarer, a **curse**: a quarter of your stamina, healing at a quarter strength with worse bleeding (Bad blood:
     double scrap), a quarter of your health gone for good (shown in hazard stripes on your bar), slower reloads, hits
     that hurt half again as much (Fragile), or half the scrap from kills, for the rest of the run. Some never come
     together. In return, three legendary cards join the hand (legendary perks, or tiered skills with every tier at
     once), and you take two.
4. **Repeat.** Each wave is bigger and meaner than the last, and pays a little more scrap. The run ends when you go down.

When the run ends, **Full run stats** folds open under your score: kills by zombie type and by weapon, headshots,
your longest kill and biggest chain, boards nailed and repaired, damage taken and your closest call, scrap earned,
spent and sent to teammates, and every skill and perk you took.

## What's in it

**The farm.** Every run lays out a new house: its footprint, its rooms, its interior doors, its windows and where the
furniture stands, plus a new yard of cars, crates, hay bales and fences. The roof and walls fade away around you so
you can always see inside. The clock runs from afternoon into night, and the dark is where the lamps, your
flashlight and the muzzle flashes matter.

**Guns.** A 9mm handgun, a .357 revolver, a .50 hand cannon, pump, double-barrel and auto shotguns, a hunting rifle, a
battle rifle, a machine pistol, a submachine gun, an assault rifle and a light machine gun. Each one has its own
reload: mags in and out, shells one at a time, speed loaders and belts. At the workbench you can add sights, lasers,
suppressors, stocks, grips and underbarrel grenades, and change calibres with conversion kits such as AK mags for the
battle rifle or 10mm for the SMG. You can also load special rounds (hollow points, slugs, flechettes and more) and
tune damage, fire rate, recoil and magazine size.

**Melee.** An ash bat, crowbar, pipe wrench, machete, katana, fire axe and sledgehammer, each with its own swings,
reach and stamina cost. Sharp blades take heads and limbs off.

**Fog rounds.** Now and then a wave comes in under a thick ground fog that rolls in off the fields. It stays out
of the house, parts round you in a tight ring that thins as it gets close, and anything out in it can't be seen,
outlined or picked out by your crosshair until it's nearly on you. Lamps glow in it, and your flashlight's beam cuts
through it.

**Zombies.** Shambling walkers, fast runners, spitters that lob acid at anyone who stands still, and brutes that
charge through fences and plough through the crowd. Shots land where they hit: a leg shot slows them, a severed leg
leaves a crawler, and a headshot drops them. From wave 4 some turned wearing armour: soldiers in helmets and vests,
SWAT in plate carriers, and riot officers whose face shields blunt headshots; builders' hard hats stop a little from
the start. Armour soaks up part of each hit where it's worn (heavier rounds, slugs and piercing rounds go through more
of it), thin early on and thicker in later waves and against a better gun, and those hits show in steel with a
shield. Enough hits crack a piece, and then it splits and falls off. The dead fall as ragdolls.

**Holding the house.** Nail up to three boards over every window and door, and repair them between waves. Upgrade the
sawhorse to a metalwork bench and bar the openings with steel grates instead, reinforced in five tiers. Vault through
windows and hop fences to escape, and shove zombies off you when they get close.

**Stations.**

| Station | What it's for |
| --- | --- |
| Workbench | Gun upgrades, attachments, ammo |
| Sawhorse / metalwork bench | Planks and metal, and bracing the boards |
| Medicine cabinet | Bandages and health |
| Ham radio | Calls in a supply crate (ammo, health, bandages, planks) that grows with the wave, as does its price, once every 5 waves |
| Gun rack | New guns for your slots |
| Strongbox (co-op) | Gives scrap to a teammate |

**Co-op.** Up to four players. On the title screen press **Co-op**, enter your name and **Host a game**. Your friends
then enter the four-letter code and press **Join**. Each player keeps their own weapons, scrap, drops and cards. If
you go down, a low drone drifts in while you watch a teammate until the wave is cleared, then come back as a new
survivor with a full mag in each gun and three spares. Clear a wave with nobody down and everyone gets a little
scrap. You can add friends by their friend code to see when they're online and join their lobby in one click.

These are the defaults. Every key except walking can be changed in **Settings → Controls**, either to another key or
to a middle or side mouse button. The keys shown in the game always match your choices.

Touch screens get twin sticks and on-screen buttons.

**First person.** Press **N** (or pick it in **Settings → Camera**) to see out of your own eyes instead of from above.
Click once to take the mouse, then look anywhere, up and down too: W A S D walk you forward, back and to the sides of
where you face. The gun and your arms move inside a small circle round the middle of the view: only a little of the
mouse's movement moves the aim in it (the view turns with the rest, and with all of it once the aim's at the edge), the
aim easing after it, and the view drifting back onto it; the crosshair is wherever the gun points. Hold **Alt** to
look round freely while the gun stays where it's aimed, and the view eases back onto it when you let go. The view
rides your head as you walk, vault and stumble, well smoothed, bobs and sways with each stride (more running), banks a
little into a sidestep, dips a touch going forward and widens a little at a sprint; shots kick it up, and a reload's beats, a pump or bolt being worked and hits rock it gently. Looking up, your
head goes back so the raised gun stays out in front of your eyes. Raising the sights is a spring onto them, starting
gently, quick through the middle and settling softly, with a little dip and cant on the way, quicker with a handgun;
a shouldered gun (the AR most) jolts and shakes out a little as the stock meets your shoulder. It brings your eye onto
the gun's own sights as the crosshair goes and the free-aim circle closes. Every gun's iron sights are lined up the way
a real shooter holds them: a handgun's front post sits level in the rear notch, the AR, battle rifle, LMG, MP5 and auto
shotgun's front post sits in the middle of the hole through the rear aperture, the whole rear sight too close to your
eye to see sharp (a soft ghost ring of it round the hole), and the pump and double barrel are aimed down the rib with
the bead sat on the receiver. An optic on the MP5 sits on a claw mount, clear over its drum sight.
The red dot's dot and the holographic sight's reticle (an EOTech 512's ring with its four ticks and centre dot, kept small inside the window,
grainy red light, slim body with its thin hood loop over the window) are drawn out at the target, where your eye sees
them, inside the sight's window, turning with the gun as it cants. A scope (the hunting scopes, the ACOG) sits a
proper eye relief out from your eye and shows its own magnified view filling the eyepiece whenever you can see it:
the picture and the reticle (a duplex, or the ACOG's lit chevron and drop marks, turning with the gun) both sit on
where the scope's axis points, so with your eye off the axis they slide together against the eyepiece's ring (real
parallax), with colour fringing and darkening toward the rim and the tube's shadow closing in from the side your eye's
off it (nearly black at the hip, opening up as it comes to your eye; a shot's kick and a quick turn swing it). Anything
in front of the eyepiece (your hands, a window frame as you vault) hides it, and nothing's drawn for a gun that's put
away. Through a scope the rest of the view goes out of focus, the gun a little. Every sight's glass is clear to look through in first person; aiming a dot or a holo keeps the gun a natural distance
from your eye, even aiming straight up. Zombies show the moment any of one is in a clear line from your eyes, round a
corner or behind you as you turn, with no fading in. Everything works from first person: picking cards between waves
(the mouse is freed for them and taken back with your pick), and the stations, the settings and the cheat menu all
hand the mouse back as they close. Esc lets go of the mouse and pauses; resuming takes it straight back without a click,
and a station or a menu lets go of it for you, closing it taking the mouse back. You go back to the view from above whenever you're down.
At the hip the gun sits out in front of you, a little right of the middle (a short gun further out, so it's never
tucked under your chin), and each shot turns it in your hands as well as kicking the view; through a scope the
picture rocks a little with it. Reloads are the same ones you see from above, only calmer from your own eyes: the gun
comes up a touch and turns a little toward you, not over on its side. The flashlight points down the gun's barrel,
easing after it, and smoke and sparks thin out near the middle of your sights so they don't hide what you're aiming
at. Coming over a fence or a sill, the view turns smoothly to where you're looking once your hands are free.

**Right-handed.** Your survivor shoots right-handed: the right hand on the grip and trigger, working the hunting
rifle's bolt and the charging handle, and the left on the handguard or the pump, fetching mags and feeding shells,
rounds and the revolver's speed loader. A speed loader dumps every round in the cylinder, spent or not, before the
fresh six go in. Between loading shells and working the pump, the bolt or the cylinder, the hands carry on from one
into the next without a jump: after the last shell the hand goes straight back to the pump, and the hand on the bolt
eases back to the grip.

## Working on the game

```bash
npm install
npm run dev        # builds and serves at http://localhost:5173, rebuilding on every save
npm run build      # minified build into dist/
npm run build:dev  # readable build with a source map, so errors point at src/js/<file>:<line>
npm run fix-imports # rewrites every file's imports and exports to match what it uses (after moving code about)
```

Every build also writes `dist/standalone.html`, the whole game in one file. Every push runs CI: all three builds and a
browser smoke test (`tools/smoke.mjs`) that starts a run, moves, shoots, reloads and pauses.

**F9** opens the cheat menu, for testing on your own (never in co-op). Its search box looks through every cheat at
once, and the switches you reach for most sit along the top: god mode, endless scrap and ammo, a bottomless magazine,
one-hit kills, waves off, and the game's speed (¼× to 2×). Its sections cover you and your supplies, health and every
status effect, weapons (any of them, turning in 3D with its numbers, kept in view at the top as you scroll down its
upgrades, rounds and mods so you see each part go on), your character (every part of your look: skin, face, eyes, hair and beard, hat, top, trousers, shoes,
gloves, wrists, pack and holster, each with its stock colours or any colour at all, shown on a copy of you in a little
studio whose camera glides in to whatever you're changing, and kept for new runs if you like), skills and perks, curses
(taken, lifted, or a sacrifice promised for the next hand), zombies (their minds hunting, passive or switched off, and
their speed, damage and health; any kind in any outfit sent in at the fence or right in front of you; their armour
cracked or broken off; the waves) and the world (the camera, the hour, fog). Under the camera there's a free camera
as well, to fly about and watch the farm from anywhere: W A S D where it looks, Space and Ctrl up and down, Shift
faster, the wheel its speed, and every zombie shown wherever it is; N goes back. The switches are remembered between visits;
everything else acts on the run you're in.

### How the code fits together

The game is plain JavaScript and three.js (r186, WebGL2), with no framework. The build bundles just the parts of
three.js the game uses into `vendor/three.min.js`. Each file in `src/js` is an ES module:

- A file imports what it uses from the other files and exports what they use from it, so the top of a file lists
  everything it depends on, and an editor can jump to where anything is defined.
- Another file's variable can only be read. The few that other files change come with a setter in their own file
  (`setShake`, `setZoom` and so on).
- The build checks every file's imports and exports (`tools/modules.mjs`). It fails, naming the file and line, on:
  - a name that isn't imported;
  - an import of something that isn't exported, or an import that isn't used;
  - an export no other file imports, or anything declared and never used;
  - an assignment to another file's variable;
  - a file using, as it loads, something from a file that loads after it.
- To move code from one file to another, move it and run `npm run fix-imports`: it rewrites the imports and exports
  of every file to match.
- For the browser, the build joins the files in the order of `src/js/load-order.json` into one script. That order only
  matters for code that runs at load time, such as building the world. The build also checks the joined script and
  names the file and line of any error, such as two files declaring the same name.
- Each frame, `game/loop.js` runs `update(dt)` (`game/update.js`, the simulation) and then `visuals(dt)`
  (`game/visuals.js`, animation, camera and HUD). Those two files list every system in order, so they're the place to
  start reading.

```
src/
  index.html     the page: HUD, menus, settings, station panels
  styles/        CSS, one file per part of the screen
  js/
    core/        renderer, shaders, maths, geometry helpers, shadow batching
    world/       the farm: house, rooms, yard, props, collision, cut-away
    lighting/    light map, day and night, fog of war
    player/      movement, aiming, vaulting, bandages, gear
    weapons/     gun stats, models, mods, firing, reloading
    melee/       melee weapons and swings
    zombies/     types, spawning, pathfinding, damage, ragdolls
    barricades/  boards and grates
    stations/    workbench, sawhorse, cabinet, radio, gun rack, strongbox
    upgrades/    the cards and tiers
    effects/     post-processing (SSAO, bloom), blood, particles, outlines
    ui/          HUD, crosshair, settings, menus
    input/       keyboard, mouse, touch and key bindings
    audio/       sounds and reverb
    game/        shared state, the loop, the camera
    net/         co-op over WebRTC
tools/           build, smoke test, and the asset packing scripts
assets/          the raw texture and sound packs (not shipped)
```

Each source file starts with a comment saying what it holds. The main shared state is `P` (the player), `W8` (your
weapons and ammo), `S` (your upgrade stats), `run` (the current run), `zombies`, and `state` and `phase` (which screen
is showing, and whether it's a wave or a break).

### Adding a gun

1. Add its stats to `GUN_WEAPONS` in `weapons/gun-stats.js`. Give it a rarity (`rar`) and a `desc`, and it will turn
   up as a card.
2. Add a model builder with the same id to `GUN_MODELS` in `weapons/gun-models.js`. Copying the closest gun is the
   easiest start.
3. Optionally add mods for it in `weapons/gun-mods.js`, a range drop-off in `weapons/ammo.js` and an ejection style in
   `weapons/casings.js`.

Melee weapons work the same way, in `melee/melee-stats.js` and `melee/melee-models.js`.
