# Farmhouse Holdout

An isometric zombie survival roguelike that runs in the browser, built with three.js.

*Kessler Farm. Board up. Prepare for the horde. Survive as long as you can.*

## Quick start

You need [Node.js](https://nodejs.org) 18 or newer.

```bash
npm install
npm run dev      # plays at http://localhost:5173 and rebuilds every time you save a file
npm run build    # production build into dist/
```

| Command | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `npm run dev` | Readable build plus a local server. Save any file in `src/` and refresh the browser. |
| `npm run build` | Minified build: `dist/index.html`, `dist/game.js`, `dist/game.css`, `dist/vendor/three.min.js`, `dist/vendor/peerjs.min.js`, `dist/sounds/`. |
| `npm run build:dev` | Same files as `build`, but not minified and with a `// ==== path` banner before each source file, so errors point at the right file. |

Every build also writes `dist/standalone.html`: the whole game in one file you can open by double-clicking it or send to someone. It loads three.js from a CDN, so it needs an internet connection; the sounds are inside it.

## Co-op

Up to four players, online. On the title screen press **Co-op**, put in your name and **Host a game**: you get a
four-letter code. Your friends open the game, press **Co-op**, type the code and **Join** (or open the page with
`?join=CODE` on the end of the address). When everyone's in, the host presses **Start together**.

- The host's game runs the farm: the zombies, the waves, the boards and the clock. Everyone else's shows what the
  host's has. Each player's survivor, health, weapons, scrap and cards are their own.
- Zombies go for whoever is nearest. Waves are 35% bigger for each extra player.
- A player who goes down watches a teammate for the rest of the wave (E or a right click for the next one still standing, Q or a left click for the one before). Their HUD gives
  way to the teammate's: a "Down" stamp up top with who they're watching, the teammate's health, planks, scrap and
  kills, and their weapon, ammo, reload and bandages; the wave count stays. The roof and walls fade round whoever's
  being watched, the same as round you. The moment the wave's last zombie falls and the
  slow motion has played out, they're back in the farmhouse at full health as a new survivor (a new face and clothes),
  with every weapon they had (mods and attachments too) but no ammo: the mags are empty and nothing's in reserve, so
  the melee weapon comes out. Then they pick a card like everyone else. The run ends when everyone is down.
- The first break doesn't start counting down until everyone has loaded onto the farm. After a wave the next break
  waits until everyone has picked their card, and on every fifth wave the bonus hand waits for everyone too. Once
  you've picked, the clock keeps running down above your card while you wait, so you can see how long the others
  have left. Pausing doesn't stop the farm.
- Teammates are outlined in their colour, with a dot over their heads. When one is off the screen, the dot waits at
  the screen's edge with an arrow pointing their way. Their health, planks, scrap and kills sit over your own health
  in the bottom left, in smaller bars.
- You see each teammate in the gear their upgrades gave them (armour, boots, knee pads, gloves, glasses, night
  vision, the helmet, the hammer or nail gun, the canteen, pills, armband, bandana, stopwatch and the second bandage
  pouch, with the rolls in it for the bandages they carry), and their laser shining from the gun in their hands.
- You see each teammate's own weapons, attachments and all: what they hold, what's stowed on their pack and hip,
  swaps, reloads (the mag out, in the hand, seated), the mags and speed loaders they drop, and the slides, bolts and
  pumps working. Gore is shared: a head split open, crushed or shot through, a limb or forearm taken off, and bullet
  holes show on everyone's screen (the holes land in roughly the same place, not exactly).
- Drops are your own. Whatever a zombie you kill drops (ammo, planks, bandages) is only on your farm, so nobody
  else can pick it up. If it dropped ammo, anyone who helped kill it (an assist, below) gets an ammo box of their
  own, on their farm, for their guns; picking yours up doesn't touch theirs.
- Scrap is your own. A zombie you kill puts its scrap straight into yours, so nobody else can take it. Hurt a
  zombie for at least a fifth of its health and it dies to a teammate within 8 seconds, and you get 1 scrap for the
  assist (2 for a brute). When a wave is cleared, everyone gets 2 scrap for each teammate still standing.
- To give scrap away, use the **strongbox** in the corner by the workbench (it's only there in co-op). Pick who gets it (click
  them), set how much with − and +, or 5, 10, 25, Half or All, and press **Give** (or Enter). The scrap
  flies off to their card and lands in their count, and they get a note saying who sent it.
- The host should keep the game's tab in view: a browser stops a hidden tab, and the farm stops with it for everyone.
- Players connect directly to each other over WebRTC (PeerJS, in `dist/vendor/`). PeerJS's free public server only
  introduces them. If it's down, run your own (`npx peerjs --port 9000`) and add `?peer=yourhost:9000` to everyone's
  address. Some strict networks block direct connections; there's no relay server for those.
- `?net=local` connects tabs of the same browser instead, with no network at all. It's useful for testing.

**Friends.** The Co-op panel has your friend code (six letters, kept in your browser). Add a friend by theirs, and you're
added to their list too. Each friend shows as away, online, in a lobby, hosting (with how full it is) or in a run. **Join**
takes you straight into a friend's lobby. While you host, **Invite** sends them yours. Anyone in your lobby who isn't a
friend yet has an **Add friend** button. You can be reached at your code whenever the game is open.

## Deploying to Netlify

1. In Netlify, choose **Add new site → Import an existing project** and pick this repository.
2. Leave the settings as they are. `netlify.toml` already sets the build command (`npm run build`), the publish folder (`dist`) and Node 20.
3. Every push to `main` deploys automatically.

## Where everything lives

```
src/
  index.html          page markup: HUD, menus, station panel, touch buttons
  styles/             CSS, one file per part of the screen (index.css imports them all)
  js/
    load-order.json   the order the game files are joined in (see "How the code fits together")
    core/             renderer, camera, shader patch, maths and geometry helpers
    world/            the map: grid, ground, house, fence, trees, props, collision, wall cut-away
    lighting/         light map, day/night cycle, fixed lights, fog of war
    characters/       the blocky human rig the player and zombies share
    player/           player state, movement, aiming, vaulting, shoving, bandages, gear, animation
    weapons/          guns: stats, models, attachments, mods, firing, reloading, casings, explosives
    melee/            melee weapons: stats, models, mods, swinging and its animation, stamina, swing trail
    zombies/          zombie types, spawning, AI, pathfinding, damage, death falls
    waves/            how each wave is built and scheduled
    upgrades/         the skills, perks and aid you pick after a wave, gun and character tiers
    stations/         workbench, sawhorse, medicine cabinet, radio, gun rack, strongbox, supply drops
    barricades/       boarding doors and windows
    pickups/          ammo, scrap, medkits and other drops
    effects/          blood, particles, smoke, tracers, muzzle flash, outlines
    ui/               HUD, crosshair, health line, status icons, rounds display, menus
    audio/            the recorded sounds (footsteps from where they are), the reverb inside the house, synthesized ones for the rest
    input/            keyboard, mouse and touch controls
    game/             shared state, the update and draw order, camera, main loop
    net/              co-op: the connection and lobby, teammates' survivors, keeping everyone's farm the same
assets/               raw packs to pick from, not shipped with the game (sizes to keep to: assets/README.md)
  textures/           texture packs (Kenney zips as they come)
  sounds/             sound packs and single sound files
  src/sounds/         (under src/) the recorded sounds the game ships, made from the packs in assets/sounds/
tools/build.mjs       the build script (copies src/sounds into dist/, versioned, and inlines it in standalone.html)
tools/make-sounds.mjs cuts, trims and levels the recordings it uses (the guns-and-stuff pack, Snake's pack and loose uploads) into src/sounds/*.ogg; name sounds to rebuild only those (needs ffmpeg and unzip)
tools/pack-footsteps.py  packs assets/sounds/Footsteps.zip into src/sounds/footsteps.mp3 and its clip list (needs ffmpeg, numpy)
tools/pack-particles.py  packs the particle sprites used out of assets/textures (Kenney Particle Pack, Light Masks) into src/js/effects/particle-art.js (needs pillow)
tools/pack-ground.py     packs the ground textures used out of assets/textures (SBS Tiny Texture Packs, read with bsdtar or unar) into src/js/world/ground-art.js, graded toward the farm's colours (needs pillow)
netlify.toml          Netlify settings
```

Every source file starts with a one-line comment saying what is in it.

### Adding a new gun

1. Add its stats to `GUN_WEAPONS` in `weapons/gun-stats.js`. Give it a `rar` (`'u'` uncommon, `'r'` rare, `'e'` epic, `'l'` legendary; `'c'` common is kept for aid cards) and a `desc`, and it appears as a card after a wave automatically.
2. Add a model builder with the same id to `GUN_MODELS` in `weapons/gun-models.js`. Copying the closest existing gun is the easiest start.
3. Optional: give it a range drop-off in `FALLOFF` (`weapons/ammo.js`) and an ejection style in `EJECT` (`weapons/casings.js`).
4. Optional: add mods for it in `weapons/gun-mods.js` with `w: '<your gun id>'`. They show up under Upgrades at the workbench while you hold it.

Adding a melee weapon works the same way, using `melee/melee-stats.js` and `melee/melee-models.js`.

## How the code fits together

The game is plain browser JavaScript with no framework. The build joins every file listed in `src/js/load-order.json`, in that order, and wraps them in one function. So:

- All files share one scope. Any file can use a function or constant from any other file without importing it.
- Function declarations work from anywhere, whatever the order.
- Order matters only for code that runs straight away at the top level, such as building the world or creating a material that another file uses at load time. If you see `Cannot access 'X' before initialization`, move the file that defines `X` higher in `load-order.json`.
- A new `.js` file must be added to `load-order.json`. The build stops with a message naming any file you forgot.

Each frame, `game/loop.js` calls `update(dt)` in `game/update.js` (the simulation, one system after another) and then `visuals(dt)` in `game/visuals.js` (animation, camera, lighting, effects and HUD). Those two files list every system in the order it runs, so they are a good place to start reading.

Shared state:

| Name | Holds | Defined in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `P` | the player | `player/player.js` |
| `S` | your character's upgrade stats | `game/run-state.js` |
| `W8` | weapons you carry, ammo, mods, per-gun tiers | `game/run-state.js` |
| `run` | the current run: wave, score, time | `game/run-state.js` |
| `zombies`, `ragdolls` | live zombies, and bodies that are falling | `zombies/zombie-types.js`, `zombies/deaths.js` |
| `state`, `phase` | which screen is showing, wave or break | `game/state.js` |

## Performance

- **One bundle.** The production build is a single minified `game.js` (about 630 KB) and one `game.css`. The page asks for both by a hash of their contents (`game.js?v=…`), so a new deploy reaches players straight away instead of waiting out the browser cache. three.js ships as its own file and is cached for a year.
- **Adaptive resolution.** The game measures frame time and lowers or raises the render resolution between 1× and your screen's pixel ratio to keep the frame rate smooth (`game/loop.js`).
- **Vision caching.** Line of sight is recomputed only when you move, turn far enough or a board changes, and at most every 0.6 seconds otherwise (`lighting/vision.js`).
- **Light map instead of real lights.** Lamps, fires and muzzle flashes are painted into one small light texture each frame instead of using many three.js lights (`lighting/lightmap.js`). It's read with a cubic B-spline (`lmCubic` in `core/shaders.js`), and the fixed lamps' shadows are softened by testing four points in each sample (`SOFT_PTS`), so pools of light and shadow edges are smooth ramps; the house's bulbs and its low fill are in `lighting/static-lights.js`. The flashlight alone is worked out per pixel in the same shader, from a few uniforms, so its beam stays smooth as you turn.
- **Pooling and instancing.** Particles, casings and tracers reuse fixed pools, and trees, grass, bushes, rocks and fence posts are instanced meshes, so long fights don't create garbage or extra draw calls.
- **Only what you can see.** A zombie or a body you can't see is left out of the frame entirely rather than drawn invisibly, and the see-through outlines aren't drawn on zombies too far off to show them. In a crowd that's about a third of the draw calls (`zombies/zombie-visuals.js`).
- **Blood in one draw.** Every flying blood droplet is an instance of one mesh, so a shotgun emptied into a crowd costs one draw call for its blood, not hundreds (`dropIM` in `effects/particles.js`).
- **Blood in one texture.** Blood on the farm isn't separate decals: every splash is drawn into one map on the GPU, and the ground, walls, fences and trees read it in their shader, so a farm covered in blood costs no more to draw than a clean one (`effects/blood-map.js`).
- **Cached geometry.** Box shapes are shared through a cache instead of being created per part (`core/geometry.js`).
- **No z-fighting.** As models are built, faces of different pieces that would sit exactly on top of each other are pulled apart by 0.6 mm (`unfight` in `core/geometry.js`).
- **Merged detail.** The small pieces of the survivor, the furniture and the stations are merged into a few meshes each, with their colours baked into the vertices, so all that detail costs only a few extra draw calls (`mergeInto` in `core/geometry.js`).

## Controls

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| W A S D | Walk in eight directions |
| Shift (hold) | Sprint (uses stamina). Run into an open window or a fence to vault it; aim or fire to skid to a stop; you can reload at a sprint with anything but the LMG (the reload up to a fifth slower the faster you're going, `runRelK`, and you run only a touch slower: `RUN_RELOAD` in `src/js/player/sprint.js`), and with the LMG starting a sprint cuts the reload short; move the cursor out to the side or behind to glance back over your shoulder |
| Mouse | Aim; click to fire a handgun (one shot a click), hold to fire an automatic or swing. With a gun in each hand, left click fires the left one and right click the right. Clicking during a bandage stops it |
| Hold right click | Aim down the sights (scoped guns zoom in further and steady right down) |
| 1 2 3 4 | Melee, sidearm, long gun, automatic (during a bandage: stops it and takes that weapon out) |
| R | Reload |
| Q | Apply a bandage (a ring round the cursor shows it going on, then the healing left) |
| V | Shove the zombies in front of you with your weapon (stabs with a bayonet fitted) |
| F (hold) | Board up the door or window you're at |
| C (hold) | Tear the top board off the door or window you're at (it breaks: you don't get the plank back) |
| Space | Climb through an open window or hop a fence (costs stamina, more if you chain them) |
| E | Use the workbench, sawhorse, medicine cabinet, radio, gun rack or (in co-op) the strongbox |
| H | Co-op: share a gun you've put down with your team, or stop sharing it (standing at it) |
| G | Underbarrel grenade, once you have one |
| B | Switch fire mode: the assault rifle between full auto and semi, and guns with a burst or full-auto mod (single → burst → full auto) |
| X | Load the next kind of round you own for the gun in your hands (it empties the gun and reloads) |
| Wheel, + and − | Zoom in and out |
| T | Start the run (also from the death screen), or the next wave early |
| Esc or P | Pause |
| M | Back to the main menu from the death screen |
| F9 | Cheat menu, for testing (deploy previews and local builds only, never the live game on main): god mode, infinite scrap and ammo, health up or down, any status effect, any weapon, a gun's upgrades, mods and rounds up or down, skills given or taken away, waves off, and the time of day |

Touch screens get twin sticks and on-screen buttons.
